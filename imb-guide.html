Field guide

Understand what the 65 bars actually encode.

Barcode Inspector translates the four-state USPS Intelligent Mail pattern into its numeric value, tracking fields, routing fields, and physical barcode information.

What is an Intelligent Mail barcode?

The USPS Intelligent Mail barcode, commonly called an IMb, is a 65-bar postal barcode used on letters, cards, and flats. USPS uses it to support automated mail sorting, identification, routing, and participating mail services.

Each bar has one of four possible states:

CodeBar stateAppearance
FFullExtends above and below the center tracker region
AAscenderExtends above the tracker region
DDescenderExtends below the tracker region
TTrackerOccupies only the center region

The 65 bars encode a 20-digit tracking code plus an optional routing code. The complete decoded value can contain up to 31 digits.

The tracking portion

The tracking portion always contains 20 digits. Barcode Inspector separates it into the fields below.

FieldLengthWhat it represents
Barcode ID2 digitsIdentifies barcode processing information, including optional endorsement-line information where applicable.
Service Type ID3 digitsIdentifies the mail class and requested services, such as address correction or other electronic service options.
Mailer ID6 or 9 digitsIdentifies the mail owner or mailing agent associated with the barcode.
Serial Number9 or 6 digitsProvides the mailpiece-specific portion. Its length complements the Mailer ID so the two fields total 15 digits.

The routing portion

An IMb may contain no routing digits or may include 5, 9, or 11 routing digits.

LengthRouting information
0 digitsNo destination routing code is encoded.
5 digitsDestination ZIP Code.
9 digitsDestination ZIP+4 Code.
11 digitsDestination ZIP+4 Code plus the two-digit delivery point.

What Barcode Inspector reports

  • Complete Barcode: the full decoded numeric value.
  • USPS Tracking Data: the 20-digit tracking portion and its component fields.
  • Routing Data: the optional destination routing portion and its interpretation.
  • Physical Barcode: the detected four-state bar representation and scan information.
  • Additional Data: other interpretations available for the decoded result.
Tracking code is not the same as tracking history. The IMb contains identifiers and requested-service information. Barcode Inspector decodes what is printed in the barcode; it does not retrieve USPS mailpiece scan events or delivery history.

Why a scan may fail

All 65 bars matter. A missing, merged, blurred, covered, or badly distorted bar can prevent a reliable decode. Try these corrections:

  • Flatten the mailpiece and keep the barcode away from glare.
  • Make sure both ends of the barcode are visible.
  • Move the camera slightly farther away to improve focus.
  • Use even lighting or enable the flashlight.
  • Try scanning a sharp photo if the live camera struggles.
